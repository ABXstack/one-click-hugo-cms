  - id: "risks"
    heading: "The Risks Are Already Here"
    image: "/img/bc-pcrb/02-wildfire-smoke-ridge.jpeg"
    alt: "A wall of wildfire smoke rising from a forested ridge in British Columbia."
    body: |
      **Not a conference. Not a political rally. A briefing for British Columbians about the risks ahead.**

      We don't have to imagine climate change here. We're already living it.

      Wildfire smoke has become part of summer. Communities have faced unprecedented heat, destructive flooding, drought and evacuation. Our forests, rivers, coastlines, farms and towns are changing.

      But the individual disasters are only part of the story. Scientists are increasingly looking at how changes in climate, oceans, forests and freshwater interact — and what happens when those pressures arrive at the same time.

      So the question isn't really whether the climate is changing.

      **It's what these changes could mean for the place we call home over the next fifty years.**

      ### Why a provincial briefing?

      There's a gap between what Earth-system science is telling us and what most of us ever hear about it. This briefing exists to close that gap.

      ### Who it's for

      Not only MLAs. British Columbians generally: seniors, families and communities; Indigenous communities; educators and students; local and provincial decision-makers; business and labour; farmers; health professionals; and media.

      ### Who will speak

      Researchers from BC universities, Indigenous knowledge holders and professionals working in the field.

      ### What they'll be asked

      *Based on your field of expertise, what are the most important risks British Columbians need to understand about the next 50 years — and what should we be preparing for now?*

      ### What happens afterwards

      Briefing &rarr; documentary &rarr; community screenings &rarr; schools &rarr; a continuing public conversation &rarr; action.

  - id: "briefing"
    heading: "A Briefing for British Columbians"
    image: "/img/bc-pcrb/03-researchers-in-forest.jpeg"
    alt: "Two researchers working with equipment in dense BC rainforest."
    body: |
      We're inviting trusted BC scientists, researchers, Indigenous knowledge holders and field professionals to speak candidly about the risks they see in their own areas of expertise.

      Wildfire and smoke. Extreme heat. Water and drought. Flooding. Forests and biodiversity. Oceans. Food systems. Human health. Insurance and economic risk.

      We'll ask each of them the same question:

      **"What do British Columbians most need to understand now about the risks facing our province over the next fifty years?"**

      The purpose isn't to tell British Columbians what political choices to make. It's to help all of us better understand the risks those choices rest on.

  - id: "science-story"
    heading: "From Science to Story"
    image: "/img/bc-pcrb/08-river-canyon.png"
    alt: "A turquoise river running through a forested BC canyon."
    body: |
      A scientific briefing can tell us what is happening. A story can help us understand why it matters.

      Climate change isn't one problem. It's a set of connected Earth systems — the ones our communities, our economies and our health all depend on — changing at once.

      ### The science

      **A warming BC** — Extreme temperatures and shifting seasons.

      **Wildfire and smoke** — Fire conditions, forest health, air quality and what they do to us.

      **Water** — Drought, snowpack, glaciers, watersheds and flooding.

      **Oceans and coasts** — Warming, acidification, sea-level impacts and fisheries.

      **Food and agriculture** — Food security, and agricultural pollution from diesel through to fertilizer runoff.

      **Health** — Air pollution, plastics, heat-related illness and mental health.

      **Economic and insurance risk** — Insurers and economists are increasingly factoring climate-related risk into costs and coverage.

      ### The story

      Moments from the Briefing, together with stories from British Columbians, will become ***Under BC's Smoky Skies*** — a short documentary about a province changing in front of us, and the choices facing the people living here now.

      The film brings together the science and the lived experience: researchers who study the changes, people living through them, and voices from different generations asking what kind of British Columbia we want to pass on.

      This isn't simply a film about climate change.

      **It's a story about British Columbia** — its landscapes, its communities, its people. A documentary about the province we're leaving behind, and the future we can still help shape.

      Smoke has become part of summer. Rivers run low. Communities evacuate. Heat records fall. Behind those events is a larger story that's hard to see against a blue sky.

      *Under BC's Smoky Skies* brings together scientists, elders, young people and British Columbians to ask what's happening to our province, where it could lead, and what one generation owes the next.
